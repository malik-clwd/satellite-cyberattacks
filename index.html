ATTACK 1

Trajectory Manipulation (Orbital Hijacking)

Vector: Exploitation of unauthenticated telecommand uplinks or compromised ground station keys to fire on-board chemical/electric thrusters.

Consequences: Satellite is pushed out of its allocated orbital slot, creating collision hazards in Low Earth Orbit (Kessler syndrome) or inducing premature atmospheric burn-up.

Mitigation: CCSDS Space Data Link Security (SDLS) authentication with HMAC-SHA256, command threshold limits, and autonomous station-keeping reversion.

ATTACK 2

GPS / GNSS Spoofing (Position Falsification)

Vector: High-power false L1/L2 GNSS signals broadcast toward the satellite's receiver antenna, overpowering real GPS constellations with slightly altered timing delays.

Consequences: The satellite's onboard GNC calculates invalid position fixes, misdirecting earth-observation payloads and satellite crosslinks.

Mitigation: Multi-constellation GNSS receiver (GPS + Galileo), Receiver Autonomous Integrity Monitoring (RAIM), and cross-verification using optical Star Trackers.

ATTACK 3

Telemetry Data Manipulation (False Data Injection)

Vector: Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) interception on downlink signals or compromised ground network routing servers alter telemetry values before presentation.

Consequences: Ground operators are tricked into issuing incorrect emergency commands (e.g., shutting down payloads believing battery is drained or CPU is overheating).

Mitigation: End-to-end cryptographic telemetry packet signatures, sensor fusion consistency checks, and Kalman filter innovation testing.

ATTACK 4

RF Communication Jamming (Denial of Service)

Vector: Ground or airborne electronic warfare transmitters flood the satellite's uplink receiver frequency with high-power Gaussian noise or chirp carriers.

Consequences: Complete loss of ground telemetry and command capabilities. Satellite drifts uncontrolled during critical mission phases.

Mitigation: Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum (FHSS), Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum (DSSS), spatial null-steering antennas, and autonomous inter-satellite laser relays.

ATTACK 5

Command Injection (Buffer Overflow & Firmware Tamper)

Vector: Exploitation of memory corruption vulnerabilities in flight software telecommand decoders to execute arbitrary payload firmware.

Consequences: Malicious actors can command reaction wheels to desaturate at max RPM, inducing violent tumble, or slew solar arrays away from sunlight to kill power.

Mitigation: Hardware-enforced Memory Protection Units (MPU), golden image bootloader rollback, and command authorization two-party rule.

💡 COMMON VIVA QUESTIONS & MODEL ANSWERS

Q: What is the difference between Jamming and Spoofing?

A: Jamming is a Denial-of-Service attack flooding the RF channel with noise so no communication gets through. Spoofing is an integrity attack broadcasting forged valid-looking navigation signals that mislead the receiver's computed position without alerting it to packet loss.

Q: Why did you choose CCSDS standards for the aerospace simulation?

A: CCSDS (Consultative Committee for Space Data Systems) is the international standard governing telemetry and telecommands used by NASA, ESA, ISRO, and commercial space operators worldwide.
